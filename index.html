One evidence chain, start to finish

How a finding can move from observation to a verified outcome.

An illustrative ASOC operating model spanning current and planned capabilities—not a claim that every step is available or connected today. Availability depends on product maturity, deployment scope, configuration, permissions, and available evidence.

  1. 01 / OBSERVED

    An unaccounted-for camera responds.

    A device missing from the current inventory appears inside an authorized discovery scope. The source, time, and scope stay attached.

  2. 02 / ENRICHED

    Identity and exposure are correlated.

    Available service and firmware signals are matched with vulnerability intelligence while confidence and coverage limits remain visible.

  3. 03 / VALIDATED

    Reachability is tested, not assumed.

    A permitted check asks whether the camera can be reached outside its intended segment and preserves the result as evidence.

  4. 04 / MODELED

    The possible path is traced.

    Analysis examines whether the exposed device could provide a route toward an operations network it was never meant to touch.

  5. 05 / WEIGHED

    Operations add the consequence.

    Asset owners connect the technical path to the systems, coverage, and continuity that could be affected.

  6. 06 / PROPOSED

    A bounded change is prepared.

    A candidate segmentation change identifies the stale rule, the intended camera boundary, and a rollback path.

  7. 07 / APPROVED

    A person authorizes the action.

    The designated owner reviews impact and rollback before any consequential production change is permitted.

  8. 08 / VERIFIED

    The same path is tested again.

    Where post-change assessment is enabled, new evidence determines whether the path closed and records what changed.

Assure and Secure apply the same evidence rules to different decisions: whether a code finding is defensible, and whether a cryptographic transition can be completed safely.
