Healthcare

Which connected clinical systems are exposed?

Bedside monitors, infusion pumps, imaging systems, and the clinical network that connects them keep patient care running, often on devices that cannot be patched or rebooted while in use. A finding that is routine on an office laptop can affect patient safety when it reaches a bedside monitor. Breakwater Secure, Assure, and SOAR give clinical engineering, hospital IT, and patient safety teams one evidence trail from exposure to a change care teams can absorb.

03GOVERN + VERIFY

Breakwater SOAR

Keep a clinical network change inside patient-safety approval.

A segmentation or access change on a clinical network carries real patient-safety stakes. SOAR opens the case with evidence attached, routes it to a named clinical engineering or biomed approver, constrains execution to a window that does not interrupt care, and re-tests the path after the change to confirm what actually closed.

  • Case evidence
  • Approval gates
  • Bounded execution
  • Outcome verification

WHAT THIS LOOKS LIKE

A bedside monitor on the wrong network, closed without interrupting care.

A bedside monitor installed during a unit renovation turns up in a routine discovery scan, still reachable from the hospital's administrative network. Secure traces its firmware to a known weakness. A permitted reachability check confirms it can reach the clinical network from outside its intended segment.

SOAR opens a case, a named clinical engineering lead approves a segmentation fix scheduled around patient rounds, and Breakwater re-tests the path after the change. The record, what was found, what was proposed, who approved it, what was confirmed, stays attached to the monitor's file.
