Beyond the individual device

Care depends on connected services, too.

Device exposure, shared access, and supplier dependencies need to be considered together. Securing a device in isolation can miss the service around it.

Medical equipment

Supplier connections

A coordinated starting point

Agree the review before the scan.

Start with one device class or department. Identify the clinical engineering team, the security owner, and the supplier’s assessment constraints.

No patient data is needed to discuss the scope. Collection methods, supported device types, and permitted actions are confirmed for the proposed evaluation.

Who needs to participate?

Security can explain the finding; clinical engineering and the care-service owner explain device use and maintenance constraints. Involve the supplier where support conditions matter.

Does visibility mean compromise?

No. A candidate vulnerability, an observed connection, and a confirmed compromise are different findings. Preserve that distinction when deciding what to investigate.

Can a device be assessed while in use?

That depends on the agreed method, device, supplier guidance, and clinical authorization. Do not assume active testing or isolation is appropriate.
